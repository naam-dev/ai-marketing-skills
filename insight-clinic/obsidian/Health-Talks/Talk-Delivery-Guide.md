# 🎯 Health Talk Delivery Guide

**Back to:** [[000-Insight-Clinic-MOC]] · [[Talk-Fatigue]] · [[Talk-Other-Topics]]

**Goal:** Everything you need to run a talk from initial booking to post-event follow-up.

> [!tip] Why talks are the highest-leverage activity
> A well-run 20-person talk should generate 2–4 new patients within 30 days. At average first consultation fee, each talk pays for itself many times over. One talk = multiple referral relationships in a single session.

---

## Step 1 — Booking Confirmation Email

Once a studio agrees to host, send this within 24 hours:

```
Subject: Health talk at [Studio] — details confirmed

Hi [Name],

Brilliant — really looking forward to it.

Here's what I need from you:
• Preferred date and time (we're flexible)
• How you'd like to promote it to your members (I'll send you copy for
  your newsletter, WhatsApp group, or Instagram — whichever works best)
• Whether you have a projector/screen or if I should bring one
• Approximate expected attendance

From my side:
• I'll bring all materials — printed cards, sign-up sheets, etc.
• The talk is 35 minutes + 10 minutes Q&A
• No cost to you or your clients
• I'll arrive 15 minutes early to set up

What date works best?

[Your name]
Insight Clinic
```

## Step 3 — Preparation Checklist

**1 week before:**
- [ ] Confirm attendance numbers with studio
- [ ] Finalise slides — use [[Talk-Fatigue]] as primary, adapt if needed
- [ ] Print: sign-up sheets (30 copies), business cards (50), referral guides (10)
- [ ] Create QR code linking to your booking page (qr-code-generator.com, free)
- [ ] Print QR code on A4 — large enough to scan from 2 metres
- [ ] Confirm AV setup (projector? TV? HDMI? Adaptor needed?)

**Day before:**
- [ ] Charge laptop
- [ ] Pack HDMI cable + adaptor (USB-C to HDMI if MacBook)
- [ ] Pack clicker or download slide advance app
- [ ] Set slide deck to presentation mode and test on external display
- [ ] Prepare 2 bottles of water
- [ ] Re-read slides once — familiar, not memorised

**Day of:**
- [ ] Arrive 15 minutes early
- [ ] Place sign-up sheets and pens on chairs or table at back
- [ ] Place business cards near exit
- [ ] Place QR code printout at the front
- [ ] Test projector/screen connection
- [ ] Set up title slide
- [ ] Greet people as they arrive — warm, not sales-y

## Step 4 — Pacing Guide

35 minutes goes faster than you think. Don't rush the first half.

| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Slides 1–2: Welcome + who you are | 3 min |
| Slides 3–9: The root causes (the substance) | 25 min |
| Slides 10–11: What to do + what we do | 5 min |
| Slide 12 + Q&A | 10 min |

**Engagement techniques:**
- Ask a show-of-hands question early (slide 3) — breaks the ice, gets people nodding
- Use "does that sound familiar?" after describing symptoms — watch for nodders
- Pause after key statistics — let them land
- Make eye contact with individuals, not just the room
- It's okay to say "I don't know" — you're not expected to know everything

## Step 5 — Handling Difficult Questions

| Question | Response |
|---|---|
| "Can you recommend specific supplements?" | "I'd rather not give specific dosages in a group setting — it really depends on the individual. Come and chat to me afterwards." |
| "What do you think about [specific diet]?" | "There are good things about most dietary approaches — the principles I've described today apply regardless of which eating style suits you." |
| "Is this covered by health insurance?" | "Some of our services are covered by certain providers — if you book a consultation, we can check for you." |
| "My GP says this isn't real / isn't worth testing for" | "GPs work within NHS guidelines, which are based on detecting disease. What we're looking for is sub-optimal function — that's a different standard. Both approaches have value." |

## Step 6 — After the Talk: Converting Attendees

The talk ends when Q&A ends. Conversion happens in the 10–15 minutes after.

> [!tip] Position yourself near the exit
> People will stop on their way out. Have your phone with booking system open.

**For people who engage you:**
1. Listen first — let them describe their situation
2. Reflect back: "So what you're describing sounds like..."
3. Name the likely root cause: "...which often points to X"
4. Offer the next step: "What I'd suggest is a discovery call so we can go through your history and tell you exactly what we'd want to look at. Do you want to do that now or shall I send you a link?"

**For people who are interested but not ready:**
> "I'll add you to our newsletter — we cover this kind of content every month."
Get their email on the sign-up sheet. Don't push.

---

## Step 7 — Post-Talk Follow-Up

**Same day:**
- Send thank-you message to studio owner: "Thank you so much for hosting — the group was brilliant. We'd love to do another one."
- Add all sign-up emails to newsletter list

**Within 48 hours:**
- Send follow-up email to sign-up list (template in [[Talk-Fatigue]])
- Log any bookings made

**1 week later:**
- Check in with the studio: "Did you get any feedback from members?" — keeps the relationship warm and plants the seed for the next talk.

## Step 8 — The Repeat Model

> [!note] Schedule the next talk before you leave the first one
> In person, after the event: *"That was brilliant — the questions were really engaged. Would it be worth doing another one in a couple of months? We could do a different topic — the gut-brain axis or hormones tend to go down really well with this kind of audience."*

**Goal:** 1–2 studio talks per month by Month 2.

### Talk Rotation Schedule

| Month | Talk | Best Studio Type |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | [[Talk-Fatigue]] — Fatigue | Pilates or yoga — any |
| Month 2 | [[Talk-Other-Topics]] — Gut-brain axis | Yoga — especially yin/restorative |
| Month 3 | [[Talk-Other-Topics]] — Hormones | Pilates or yoga — female majority |
| Month 4 | [[Talk-Other-Topics]] — Stress-inflammation | Any — urban professional audience |
| Month 5 | [[Talk-Other-Topics]] — Nutrition for active people | Pilates — most relevant |
| Month 6 | [[Talk-Fatigue]] — Fatigue again | Same studio, new members |

Rotating topics means the studio's members get varied content, you don't repeat yourself, and each talk targets a different segment.
