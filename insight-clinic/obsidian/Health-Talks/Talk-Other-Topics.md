## Talk 2 — The Gut-Brain Axis

**Full title:** "The Gut-Brain Axis — What Your Body Is Telling You"
**Best for:** Yoga audiences · restorative/yin studios
**Duration:** 35 min + Q&A
**Hook:** "Your gut makes 95% of your serotonin. Your mood isn't just in your head."

---

### Slide Structure

**Slide 1 — Title**
> The Gut-Brain Axis
> What your gut is telling your brain — and what to do about it
> Insight Clinic · insightclinic.care

**Slide 2 — Who We Are** *(same as fatigue talk)*

**Slide 3 — Opening Hook**
- The vagus nerve: the superhighway between gut and brain
- 500 million neurons in your gut — your "second brain"
- 95% of serotonin produced in the gut, not the brain
- Key question: "When did you last feel calm, clear-headed, and energised at the same time?"

**Slide 4 — The Gut Microbiome**
- 38 trillion bacteria — outnumber human cells
- Diversity = resilience; low diversity = vulnerability
- What depletes the microbiome: antibiotics, stress, ultra-processed food, alcohol, NSAIDs
- What feeds it: fibre diversity, fermented foods, polyphenols

**Slide 5 — Gut Dysbiosis and What It Causes**
Not just digestive symptoms — gut dysbiosis drives:
- Anxiety and low mood (serotonin deficit)
- Brain fog and poor concentration
- Fatigue and poor sleep
- Skin conditions (acne, eczema, rosacea)
- Autoimmune flares
- Hormonal imbalance (oestrogen recycling happens in the gut)

**Slide 6 — Leaky Gut (Intestinal Permeability)**
- What it is: gut lining becomes permeable → undigested particles enter bloodstream
- Triggers: stress, gluten (in susceptible individuals), alcohol, NSAIDs, dysbiosis
- Result: systemic inflammation → fatigue, joint pain, brain fog, food reactivity
- Measurable with: zonulin test, stool analysis

**Slide 7 — SIBO**
- Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth — often misdiagnosed as IBS
- Symptoms: bloating that builds through the day, gas, fatigue, brain fog, nutrient deficiency
- Affects 60–70% of people diagnosed with IBS
- Diagnosable with breath test; treatable with diet + botanical or antibiotic protocol

**Slide 8 — The Gut-Hormone Connection**
- The "estrobolome": gut bacteria that metabolise oestrogen
- Dysbiosis → oestrogen recycling disrupted → oestrogen dominance or deficiency
- Connects to: PMS, PCOS, endometriosis, perimenopausal symptoms

**Slide 9 — The Yoga / Movement Connection**
- Exercise increases microbiome diversity (especially aerobic + mind-body movement)
- Yoga specifically activates the vagus nerve — reducing gut-brain inflammation signalling
- Chronic stress suppresses vagal tone — yoga counteracts this directly
- The audience is already doing the best thing for their gut-brain axis

**Slide 10 — 3 Things to Do This Week**
1. Add one fermented food daily: sauerkraut, kefir, kimchi, live yoghurt (not heat-treated)
2. Eat 30 different plant foods this week — herbs, spices, nuts, seeds all count
3. Identify your "gut stressors": alcohol, gluten, dairy, processed food — trial-remove one for 2 weeks

**Slide 11 — What We Test and Treat**
- Comprehensive stool analysis (microbiome mapping, pathogen detection, leaky gut markers)
- SIBO breath test
- Food sensitivity testing
- Treatment: targeted nutrition, botanical antimicrobials, gut-repair protocols, acupuncture for gut-brain axis

**Slide 12 — Q&A + Next Steps** *(same format as fatigue talk)*

## Talk 3 — Hormones and Your Body

**Full title:** "Hormones and Your Body — Energy, Mood, and the Cycle"
**Best for:** Pilates and yoga studios with predominantly female audience
**Duration:** 40 min + Q&A
**Hook:** "Most hormonal symptoms are manageable. Most women are just never told how."

---

### Slide Structure

**Slide 1 — Title**
> Hormones and Your Body
> Energy, mood, weight, and your cycle — what's really going on
> Insight Clinic · insightclinic.care

**Slide 2 — Who We Are**

**Slide 3 — Opening**
- Show of hands: who has experienced significant PMS, period pain, fatigue around their cycle, irregular cycles, perimenopausal symptoms?
- "These are common but they are not normal. There's a difference."
- Conventional response: the pill, HRT, antidepressants. Integrative response: find out why.

**Slide 4 — The Key Hormones**
- Oestrogen: energy, mood, bone density, skin, libido
- Progesterone: calming, sleep, balances oestrogen
- Testosterone: drive, strength, confidence, libido
- Cortisol: stress, blood sugar, thyroid interaction
- Insulin: blood sugar, weight, PCOS driver

**Slide 5 — Oestrogen Dominance**
- Too much oestrogen relative to progesterone
- Causes: stress (cortisol suppresses progesterone), gut dysbiosis, xenoestrogens (plastics, pesticides), liver congestion
- Symptoms: heavy periods, PMS, breast tenderness, mood swings, weight gain hips/thighs, fibroids, endometriosis
- Measurable: DUTCH test

**Slide 6 — PCOS**
- Affects 1 in 10 women; often missed or mismanaged
- Root causes: insulin resistance (most common), adrenal PCOS, inflammatory PCOS
- Why "just take the pill" is an incomplete answer: suppresses symptoms, doesn't address root
- Integrative approach: target root cause — insulin, adrenal, or inflammatory
- Evidence-based: nutrition, inositol, N-acetyl cysteine, acupuncture

**Slide 7 — Perimenopause (The 10-Year Transition)**
- Starts on average age 42–45, not "when periods stop"
- Early symptoms often misidentified as depression, anxiety, burnout
- Brain fog, sleep disruption, mood swings, joint pain, fatigue, irregular cycles
- The window of opportunity: early intervention changes the long-term trajectory
- HRT is one option; integrative approaches address the same mechanisms differently

**Slide 8 — The Thyroid-Hormone-Fatigue Triangle**
- Thyroid, oestrogen, and adrenal cortisol all interact
- High cortisol → suppresses thyroid conversion (T4 → T3)
- Oestrogen → increases thyroid-binding globulin → less free thyroid hormone
- Result: normal TSH with significant hypothyroid symptoms
- Must test: Free T3, Free T4, thyroid antibodies

**Slide 9 — Cycle Syncing**
- Matching exercise, nutrition, and workload to menstrual cycle phases
- Follicular (days 1–14): higher energy, good for intense exercise
- Luteal (days 15–28): energy drops, need more rest, magnesium, slower practice
- Not about doing less — about doing the right things at the right time

**Slide 10 — 3 Things to Do This Week**
1. Track your cycle — note energy, mood, sleep quality, symptoms on each day for 2 months (Clue or Natural Cycles app)
2. Support your liver: reduce alcohol, increase cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, kale), add ground flaxseed daily
3. Test your hormones properly: ask for DUTCH test or book with us — one blood test on day 3 of your cycle tells you far more than "normal"

**Slide 11 — What We Test and Treat**
- DUTCH comprehensive hormone test (gold standard)
- Full thyroid panel
- Insulin and blood sugar markers
- Treatment: nutrition protocols, targeted supplements (inositol, magnesium, zinc, DIM, ashwagandha), acupuncture, naturopathy
- Works alongside and complementary to conventional HRT/contraception

**Slide 12 — Q&A + Next Steps**

## Talk 4 — The Stress-Inflammation Connection

**Full title:** "The Stress-Inflammation Connection — and What to Do About It"
**Best for:** Urban professional yoga/Pilates audience
**Duration:** 30 min + Q&A
**Hook:** "Chronic low-grade inflammation is behind almost every condition you're afraid of getting. And lifestyle is the most powerful anti-inflammatory there is."

---

### Slide Structure

**Slide 1 — Title**
> The Stress-Inflammation Connection
> Why chronic stress is making you ill — and the practical antidote

**Slide 3 — The Inflammation Primer**
- Acute inflammation: good — it's healing
- Chronic low-grade inflammation: silent, systemic, driving most chronic disease
- Linked to: fatigue, depression, autoimmune conditions, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, Alzheimer's
- Key marker: hsCRP — rarely tested in standard panels

**Slide 4 — How Stress Causes Inflammation**
- Cortisol → short-term anti-inflammatory
- Chronic cortisol → immune system desensitisation → inflammatory cytokines rise unchecked
- The paradox: chronic stress makes you simultaneously more inflamed and more immune-suppressed

**Slide 5 — The Lifestyle Drivers**
Six factors driving chronic inflammation:
1. Sleep deprivation (< 7 hours → CRP rises 25%)
2. Ultra-processed food and sugar
3. Gut dysbiosis
4. Chronic psychological stress
5. Sedentary behaviour (also overtraining without recovery)
6. Environmental toxins: plastics, air pollution, personal care products

**Slide 6 — The Anti-Inflammatory Lifestyle**
- Food: Mediterranean-style, polyphenol-rich, fibre-diverse, low refined sugar
- Top foods: olive oil, oily fish, turmeric, ginger, berries, leafy greens, green tea
- Movement: moderate intensity — yoga and Pilates are anti-inflammatory; overtraining is pro-inflammatory
- Sleep: 7–9 hours; quality matters as much as duration

**Slide 7 — Supplements with Evidence**
- Omega-3 (EPA/DHA): strongest evidence, 2–3g daily from quality fish oil
- Curcumin (turmeric extract): requires piperine for absorption (BCM-95 formulation)
- Magnesium: reduces inflammatory cytokines, supports HPA axis, improves sleep
- Vitamin D: immune modulation; most UK adults deficient in winter

**Slide 8 — Testing Your Inflammation**
- hsCRP: optimal < 0.5 mg/L (standard labs flag at > 5 mg/L — too late)
- Homocysteine: cardiovascular risk and methylation marker; rarely checked
- Ferritin: elevated ferritin (> 150) is an inflammation marker
- HbA1c: chronic blood sugar dysregulation is pro-inflammatory

**Slide 10 — 3 Things to Do This Week**
1. Add omega-3: 2g daily of quality fish oil (or algae-based if vegetarian)
2. Sleep before midnight: cortisol and growth hormone cycles mean hours before midnight are disproportionately restorative
3. Ask for hsCRP on your next blood test: if your GP won't run it, we will

**Slide 12 — Q&A + Next Steps**

## Talk 5 — Eating for Energy and Recovery

**Full title:** "Eating for Energy and Recovery — What Pilates/Yoga Students Need to Know"
**Best for:** Pilates studios specifically
**Duration:** 30 min + Q&A
**Hook:** "The most common nutrition mistake people who exercise make — and why it's making them more tired, not less."

---

### Slide Structure

**Slide 1 — Title**
> Eating for Energy and Recovery
> The nutrition mistakes that keep active people exhausted

**Slide 3 — The Paradox**
- You exercise regularly, eat well (mostly), and you're still tired
- The active person's trap: higher demand + often lower intake
- Under-fuelling is more common in Pilates/yoga communities than over-fuelling
- Fear of carbs, under-eating around training, skipping meals = compounding fatigue

**Slide 4 — Macronutrients for Active People**
- Protein: most active people eat too little; 1.6–2g per kg of bodyweight
- Carbohydrates: timing matters; fear of carbs around training is counterproductive
- Fat: essential for hormone production, joint lubrication, fat-soluble vitamins

**Slide 5 — Most Common Deficiencies in Active People**
- Iron and ferritin: especially active women; symptoms mimic overtraining
- Magnesium: depleted by exercise and stress
- Vitamin D: winter UK deficit; affects muscle function, immune regulation, mood
- B vitamins: needed for energy metabolism; depleted by stress and alcohol
- Zinc: immune function, testosterone, wound healing; lost in sweat

**Slide 6 — Timing Matters**
- Pre-workout: easily digestible carbs + small protein 60–90 min before
- Post-workout: protein + carbs within 30–60 min (muscle protein synthesis window)
- Fasted training: fine for low-intensity; depletes glycogen and cortisol for higher intensity
- Intermittent fasting and women: evidence for negative impact on female hormones with aggressive restriction

**Slide 7 — Gut Health and Absorption**
- You absorb what your gut allows, not what you eat
- Exercise-induced gut permeability: high-intensity training temporarily increases leaky gut markers
- Signs your gut isn't absorbing well: persistent deficiencies despite supplementing, bloating, fatigue after eating

**Slide 9 — Supplements Worth Considering**
- Creatine: evidence-based for strength, cognition, energy — not just bodybuilders
- Magnesium glycinate: sleep, muscle recovery, HPA axis
- Omega-3: joint health, inflammation, mood
- Vitamin D + K2: immune, bone, muscle function
- Collagen peptides: joint and connective tissue support for Pilates practitioners

**Slide 10 — 3 Things to Do This Week**
1. Track your protein for 3 days: most active women eating "healthily" hit 50–70g; target is 100–120g
2. Eat within 30 minutes of training: even nuts + banana counts
3. Add magnesium glycinate before bed (200–400mg): sleep better, recover faster, feel the difference within a week

**Slide 12 — Q&A + Next Steps**
