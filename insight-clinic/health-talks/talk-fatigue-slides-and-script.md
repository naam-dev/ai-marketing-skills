### SLIDE 5 — Root Cause 1: Thyroid

**Headline:**
> 1. Your Thyroid — and Why TSH Isn't Enough

**Bullet points:**
- Standard test: TSH only
- What's missing: Free T3, Free T4, Reverse T3, thyroid antibodies (TPO, TgAb)
- Hashimoto's thyroiditis affects 1 in 10 women — most are undiagnosed
- Symptoms: fatigue, brain fog, cold hands, weight gain, hair thinning, depression

**Visual suggestion:** Simple diagram — TSH (what GPs test) vs the full picture (Free T3, Free T4, rT3, antibodies)

**Speaker note:**
"The thyroid is your body's metabolic engine. When it's underperforming — even subclinically — everything slows down. Energy, mood, digestion, temperature regulation, hair growth.

The standard test is TSH — thyroid stimulating hormone. It's a useful marker, but it's only one piece. It's a bit like checking the fuel gauge and concluding the engine is fine.

What we check in integrative medicine is the full picture: how much thyroid hormone is actually being produced, how much is being converted into the active form, and whether the immune system is quietly attacking the thyroid — which is Hashimoto's, an autoimmune condition that's staggeringly common but frequently missed until it's quite advanced.

I see patients weekly who have been told their TSH is normal for years, who then come to us and we find significant thyroid antibodies or poor T3 conversion. Once we address that — with dietary changes, specific supplements, sometimes medication — the fatigue lifts in a matter of weeks."

### SLIDE 6 — Root Cause 2: Adrenal / HPA Axis

**Headline:**
> 2. Adrenal Dysregulation — When Your Stress System Burns Out

**Bullet points:**
- HPA axis: brain → adrenal glands → cortisol
- Chronic stress dysregulates cortisol rhythm
- Symptoms: wired-but-tired, crashing in the afternoon, needing caffeine to function, poor sleep, anxiety
- Not the same as "adrenal fatigue" (a misused term) — but HPA dysregulation is real and measurable
- Test: 4-point salivary cortisol (not a standard blood test)

**Speaker note:**
"This one is particularly relevant in a room full of people who live in London, have busy lives, and do Pilates [or yoga] — which, by the way, is brilliant for this, and we'll come back to that.

The HPA axis is the system that runs your stress response. It involves your brain, your pituitary gland, and your adrenal glands — which sit above your kidneys. When you're chronically stressed — and not all stress is obvious; overexercising, under-eating, poor sleep, emotional load — this system gets dysregulated.

The typical presentation is: you can't get going in the morning without caffeine, you get a second wind around 10pm and can't sleep, and you're exhausted between 2 and 4pm. Does that sound familiar to anyone?"

[pause]

"That's a cortisol curve problem. And it's completely measurable with a simple saliva test taken at four points during the day. Standard blood cortisol — which is occasionally tested by GPs — is taken at one point in the morning and tells you almost nothing about the curve."

### SLIDE 7 — Root Cause 3: Gut Health

**Headline:**
> 3. The Gut — Where 70% of Your Energy Story Begins

**Bullet points:**
- Gut dysbiosis: imbalance of bacteria that impairs nutrient absorption
- SIBO (small intestinal bacterial overgrowth) — causes fatigue, bloating, brain fog
- Leaky gut → systemic inflammation → fatigue
- The gut-mitochondria connection: you can eat perfectly and still be deficient if your gut isn't absorbing

**Visual suggestion:** Simple gut → bloodstream → mitochondria → energy flow diagram

**Speaker note:**
"This is the one that surprises people most. They say: 'But I eat really well.' And they probably do. But eating well only works if your gut is absorbing what you eat.

If you have gut dysbiosis — an imbalance in your gut microbiome — or SIBO, or what's sometimes called leaky gut — your ability to absorb key nutrients from food is compromised. You can be eating a perfect diet and still be significantly deficient in iron, B12, magnesium, zinc — all of which are critical for energy production.

Beyond absorption, the gut is producing neurotransmitters, regulating your immune system, and sending signals directly to your brain via the vagus nerve. An unhappy gut creates brain fog, low mood, and fatigue — even if your digestion itself seems 'fine'. Fatigue with no obvious digestive symptoms is often a gut issue."

### SLIDE 8 — Root Cause 4: Nutrient Deficiencies

**Headline:**
> 4. The Nutrients GPs Rarely Test For

**Two-column layout:**

**Standard panel:**
- Ferritin (iron stores) — often tested
- B12 — occasionally tested
- Vitamin D — sometimes tested

**What's often missed:**
- Magnesium (intracellular — serum test is useless)
- Folate (B9)
- Zinc
- CoQ10
- Omega-3 index
- B vitamins (B1, B2, B6)

**Speaker note:**
"Even when GPs do check nutrients, they often check the wrong markers or interpret the results too conservatively.

Magnesium is a great example. Serum magnesium — the standard blood test — is almost always normal, because your body will strip magnesium from your cells and bones to keep the blood level stable. Intracellular magnesium, which is what actually matters, can be severely depleted while serum levels look fine.

Magnesium is involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions in the body, including every step of ATP production — that's energy production at the cellular level. If you're low in magnesium, you're tired. Full stop. And given that stress depletes magnesium, modern urban life practically guarantees insufficiency for most people.

CoQ10 is another one. It's the spark plug of your mitochondria — the part of your cell that makes energy. It's not routinely tested, it's not in most multivitamins, and it declines significantly in your 40s and with certain medications, particularly statins."

### SLIDE 9 — Root Cause 5: Blood Sugar

**Headline:**
> 5. Blood Sugar — The Energy Rollercoaster You Might Not Know You're On

**Bullet points:**
- Standard HbA1c only flags diabetes — misses dysregulation
- Reactive hypoglycaemia: blood sugar spikes → crashes → fatigue, irritability, cravings
- Symptoms: energy dips after meals, needing to eat frequently, crashes mid-morning or afternoon, waking between 2–4am
- Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) reveals patterns invisible to standard tests

**Speaker note:**
"Blood sugar dysregulation is arguably the most common driver of energy problems that nobody talks about in the context of fatigue.

Most people think of blood sugar as a diabetes issue. But you don't need to be diabetic to have your blood sugar causing significant problems. Reactive hypoglycaemia — where blood sugar spikes and then crashes — creates a very specific pattern of fatigue: you feel okay after eating, then crash an hour or two later. You need to eat every few hours or you feel awful. You get irritable, anxious, or brain-fogged before meals.

This pattern is measurable and fixable — usually through changes to meal composition and timing, not anything complicated or extreme. But it requires someone who's looking for it."
