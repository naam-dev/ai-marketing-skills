# Health Talk Delivery Guide
**Everything you need to run a talk from initial booking to post-event follow-up**

---

## Step 1 — Booking the Talk

Once a studio agrees to host, send this confirmation email within 24 hours:

```
Subject: Health talk at [Studio] — details confirmed

Hi [Name],

Brilliant — really looking forward to it.

Here's what I need from you:
• Preferred date and time (we're flexible)
• How you'd like to promote it to your members (I'll send you copy for
  your newsletter, WhatsApp group, or Instagram — whichever works best)
• Whether you have a projector/screen or if I should bring one
• Approximate expected attendance

From my side:
• I'll bring all materials — printed cards, sign-up sheets, etc.
• The talk is 35 minutes + 10 minutes Q&A
• No cost to you or your clients
• I'll arrive 15 minutes early to set up

What date works best?

[Your name]
Insight Clinic
```

## Step 3 — Your Preparation Checklist

**1 week before:**
- [ ] Confirm attendance numbers with studio
- [ ] Finalise slides — use fatigue talk as primary, adapt if needed
- [ ] Print: sign-up sheets (30 copies), business cards (50), referral guides (10)
- [ ] Create QR code linking to your booking page (use qr-code-generator.com, free)
- [ ] Print QR code on A4 — large enough to scan from 2 metres
- [ ] Confirm AV setup with studio (projector? TV? HDMI? Adaptor needed?)

**Day before:**
- [ ] Charge laptop
- [ ] Pack HDMI cable + adaptor (USB-C to HDMI if MacBook)
- [ ] Pack clicker or download slide advance app (Keynote Remote, PowerPoint Presenter)
- [ ] Set slide deck to presentation mode and test on external display
- [ ] Prepare 2 bottles of water
- [ ] Re-read slides once — you don't need to memorise, just be familiar

**Day of:**
- [ ] Arrive 15 minutes early
- [ ] Place sign-up sheets and pens on chairs or table at back
- [ ] Place business cards near exit
- [ ] Place QR code printout at the front
- [ ] Test projector/screen connection
- [ ] Set up title slide
- [ ] Greet people as they arrive — warm, not sales-y

## Step 4 — During the Talk

**Pacing:**
- 35 minutes goes faster than you think — don't rush the first half to cram everything in
- Slides 1–2: 3 minutes (welcome, who you are)
- Slides 3–9: 25 minutes (the 5 root causes — the meat)
- Slides 10–11: 5 minutes (what to do, what we do)
- Slide 12 + Q&A: 10 minutes

**Engagement techniques:**
- Ask a show-of-hands question early (slide 3) — breaks the ice, gets people nodding
- Use "does that sound familiar?" after describing symptoms — watch for nodders
- Pause after key statistics — let them land
- Make eye contact with individuals, not just the room
- It's okay to say "I don't know" — you're not expected to know everything

**Handling difficult questions:**
- "Can you recommend specific supplements?" → "I'd rather not give specific dosages in a group setting — it really depends on the individual. Come and chat to me afterwards."
- "What do you think about [specific diet]?" → "There are good things about most dietary approaches — the principles I've described today apply regardless of which eating style suits you."
- "Is this covered by health insurance?" → "Some of our services are covered by certain providers — if you book a consultation, we can check for you."
- "My GP says this isn't real / isn't worth testing for" → "GPs work within NHS guidelines, which are based on detecting disease. What we're looking for is sub-optimal function — that's a different standard. Both approaches have value."

## Step 5 — After the Talk: Converting Attendees

The talk ends when the Q&A ends. But the conversion happens in the 10–15 minutes after.

**Position yourself near the exit** — people will stop on their way out.

**For people who engage you:**
Listen first. Let them describe their situation. Then:
- Reflect back what you heard: "So what you're describing sounds like..."
- Name the likely root cause: "...which often points to X"
- Offer the next step: "What I'd suggest is a discovery call so we can go through your history and tell you exactly what we'd want to look at. Do you want to do that now or shall I send you a link?"

**Book on the spot if possible:**
Have your phone with your booking system or calendar open. If someone's interested right now, capture it right now. "Shall we find 15 minutes this week?" is much more effective than "here's our website".

**For people who are interested but not ready:**
- "I'll add you to our newsletter — we cover this kind of content every month"
- Get their email on the sign-up sheet
- They'll book when they're ready. Don't push.

---

## Step 6 — Post-Talk Follow-Up

**Same day:**
- Send thank-you message to studio owner/manager: "Thank you so much for hosting — the group was brilliant. We'd love to do another one."
- Add all sign-up emails to your newsletter list

**Within 48 hours:**
- Send follow-up email to the sign-up list (template in `talk-fatigue-slides-and-script.md`)
- Log any bookings made and follow up with confirmation

**1 week later:**
- Check in with the studio: "Did you get any feedback from members?" This keeps the relationship warm and plants the seed for the next talk.

---

## Step 7 — Repeating the Model

**The goal is to run 1–2 studio talks per month by Month 2.**

Schedule the next talk before you leave the first one. In person, after the event:

> "That was brilliant — the questions were really engaged. Would it be worth doing another one in a couple of months? We could do a different topic — the gut-brain axis or hormones tend to go down really well with this kind of audience."

Rotating topics means:
- The studio's members get varied content (they'll keep coming)
- You don't repeat yourself
- Each talk targets a different segment of the audience

**Talk rotation schedule (suggested):**

| Month | Talk | Studio type |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | Fatigue | Pilates or yoga — any |
| Month 2 | Gut-brain axis | Yoga — especially Yin/restorative |
| Month 3 | Hormones | Pilates or yoga — female majority audience |
| Month 4 | Stress-inflammation | Any — especially urban professional audience |
| Month 5 | Nutrition for active people | Pilates — most relevant |
| Month 6 | Fatigue again | Same studio, new members |
